Cancer symptoms and treatment

Learning about possible warning signs and available care can help you have a useful conversation with a healthcare professional. Symptoms alone cannot tell whether someone has cancer.

Possible symptoms to discuss

Cancer symptoms vary by type and may also be caused by many other conditions. A new, persistent, unexplained, or worsening change deserves medical advice.

A new lump or swelling

Especially one that is growing or does not go away.

Unexplained changes in weight

Unintentional weight loss or gain without an obvious reason.

Unusual bleeding or bruising

Including blood in urine or stool, or bleeding that is not normal for you.

Changes in bowel or bladder habits

Persistent changes, trouble urinating, or ongoing constipation or diarrhea.

A sore or skin change

A sore that does not heal, or a mole or other skin mark that changes.

Ongoing cough, hoarseness, or swallowing trouble

Symptoms that persist, worsen, or have no clear explanation.

Persistent pain or fatigue

Ongoing pain or tiredness that is unusual for you and does not improve.

Other lasting changes

Persistent bloating, indigestion, fever, or night sweats can have many causes but may be worth discussing.

A symptom does not mean you have cancer. Only a qualified healthcare professional can assess your situation and recommend any needed tests.

How cancer is treated

There is no single treatment or home remedy that heals every cancer. The care plan depends on the cancer type, its location and stage, test results, and the person's overall health and preferences.

Treatments a care team may discuss

  • Surgery to remove a tumor when appropriate.
  • Radiation therapy to destroy or shrink cancer cells in a targeted area.
  • Medicines, such as chemotherapy, immunotherapy, targeted therapy, or hormone therapy.
  • Stem-cell or bone-marrow transplant for certain cancers and situations.
  • Clinical trials that study potential new treatments; eligibility and risks should be discussed with the care team.
  • Supportive or palliative care to help manage symptoms and side effects alongside other treatment, or on its own when appropriate.

Some cancers can be cured; others can be controlled for a long time or treated to improve comfort and quality of life. Your oncology team can explain the goals, likely benefits, risks, and alternatives for your specific case.

Please be cautious with claims of a guaranteed cure. Do not stop or delay prescribed treatment, or use supplements or alternative remedies in its place, without first talking with your healthcare team. Some products can interfere with treatment.

What to do next

Arrange a medical appointment

Tell a clinician about symptoms that are new, persistent, unusual, or getting worse.

Prepare notes

Write down when changes began, how they have changed, and any questions or medicines to mention.

Ask about screening

Ask whether recommended screening applies to you based on your age, health, and family history.

Get urgent care for severe or rapidly worsening symptoms. For an emergency, contact your local emergency service.
