Cancer awareness

Notice changes.
Talk with a doctor.

Knowing what is normal for your body helps you spot unusual changes early. Many symptoms can happen for reasons other than cancer, but persistent or unexplained symptoms are still worth discussing with a healthcare professional.

Possible warning signs

Changes to pay attention to

Cancer can cause different symptoms depending on the type and where it is in the body. Some cancers do not cause obvious symptoms early on. These signs are examples and are not a diagnosis by themselves.

◉

New lump or swelling

A new lump or unusual swelling anywhere on the body can be important to check, especially if it is growing or persistent.

↗

Unexplained weight change

Unintentional weight loss or weight gain without a clear reason may deserve medical review.

⌁

Ongoing pain or fatigue

Pain that does not improve or unusual tiredness that lasts for a long time should not be ignored.

◷

Changes in bowel or bladder habits

Persistent diarrhea, constipation, blood in stool, or trouble urinating may need attention.

♡

Bleeding or bruising

Unexpected bleeding, blood in urine or stool, or repeated unexplained bruising should be assessed.

☼

Skin or mouth changes

A sore that does not heal, a changing mole, or a persistent mouth patch or lesion needs evaluation.

≋

Persistent cough or voice changes

Ongoing hoarseness, swallowing problems, or a cough that continues or worsens should be checked.

⌕

Digestive discomfort

Ongoing bloating, indigestion, or trouble swallowing can happen for many reasons, but should be reviewed if it persists.

＋

Night sweats or fever

Repeated fevers or night sweats with no clear cause may be an important symptom to mention to a doctor.

One symptom does not mean you have cancer.

Many common illnesses cause similar symptoms. What matters most is noticing a new change, whether it lasts, and whether it is unusual for your body.

Your next step

If a change concerns you, speak up.

Book a visit with a healthcare professional if a symptom is new, unusual, worsening, or does not go away. You do not have to wait until symptoms become severe.

Before your appointment

  • Write down when the change started and how it has changed.
  • Note other symptoms and any new patterns in your body.
  • Ask whether screening is recommended for your age or family history.
  • If you have urgent symptoms, seek medical care right away.

Important: This page is for general education only and is not a diagnosis or substitute for professional medical advice. If you have health concerns, contact a qualified healthcare professional.
