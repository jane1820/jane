Cancer awareness

Notice changes.
Talk with a doctor.

Knowing what is usual for your body can help you notice changes and describe them to a healthcare professional. Many symptoms have causes other than cancer, but persistent or unusual changes are worth discussing.

Possible warning signs

Changes to pay attention to

Cancer can cause different symptoms depending on its type and location. Some cancers may cause no noticeable symptoms early on. These examples are not a checklist for diagnosing cancer.

◉

A new lump or swelling

A new or changing lump anywhere on the body, including the breast or testicle, should be checked.

↗

Unexplained weight change

Weight loss or gain that is not expected and has no clear reason can be worth discussing.

⌁

Ongoing pain or fatigue

Pain that does not go away, or unusual tiredness that persists, may have many possible causes.

◷

Changes in bowel or bladder habits

Persistent changes—such as ongoing constipation, diarrhea, or trouble urinating—should be assessed.

♡

Unusual bleeding or bruising

Unexpected bleeding, blood in urine or stool, or frequent unexplained bruising needs medical advice.

☼

Skin or mouth changes

A changing mole, a sore that does not heal, or a mouth patch that persists should be checked.

≋

A cough or voice change that persists

A cough, hoarseness, or trouble swallowing that continues or worsens deserves attention.

⌕

Persistent indigestion or bloating

Ongoing digestive discomfort or bloating can have many causes; seek advice if it is unusual for you.

＋

Fever or night sweats without a clear cause

Repeated or persistent episodes, especially with other unexplained changes, are worth mentioning to a clinician.

One symptom does not mean you have cancer.

Common illnesses and other conditions can cause similar symptoms. A healthcare professional can help assess what is happening and whether tests are needed.

Your next step

If a change concerns you, speak up.

Contact a healthcare professional if a symptom is new, unexplained, worsening, or does not go away. You do not need to wait until symptoms are severe.

Before your appointment

  • Note when the change started and how it has changed.
  • Write down other symptoms and any questions you have.
  • Ask whether screening is recommended for your age and personal or family history.
  • Follow local medical advice for urgent or severe symptoms.

Important: This page is for general education and is not a diagnosis or a substitute for professional medical advice. If you have concerns about your health, contact a qualified healthcare professional.
